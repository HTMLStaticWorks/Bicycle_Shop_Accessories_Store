How to Fix a Flat Tire in Under 5 Minutes

Don't let a flat ruin your ride. Follow our expert mechanic's quick guide to swapping a tube on the go.

Every cyclist, regardless of experience level, will eventually get a flat tire. It's an inevitable part of riding. But a flat doesn't mean your ride is over, nor does it mean you need to call a cab or trudge miles back to civilization.

What You'll Need

  • Tire levers (at least two)
  • A spare inner tube (the correct size for your wheel)
  • A mini pump or CO2 inflator

Step-by-Step Guide

1. Remove the Wheel

First, shift your gears to the smallest cog in the back. This makes removing the rear wheel much easier. Undo the quick release or thru-axle, pull back the derailleur slightly, and ease the wheel out of the dropouts.

2. Unseat the Tire

Let out any remaining air. Take one tire lever, wedge it under the bead of the tire, and hook it onto a spoke. Take the second lever, insert it a few inches away, and pry the bead off the rim. Slide the lever around the rim to completely unseat one side of the tire.

3. Find the Culprit

Before putting a new tube in, carefully run your fingers along the inside of the tire. Feel for thorns, glass shards, or wires that caused the puncture. If you don't remove the cause, your new tube will pop instantly.

4. Seat the New Tube

Inflate the new tube just enough to give it shape—this prevents twisting. Insert the valve into the valve hole on the rim, and tuck the tube inside the tire all the way around.

5. Remount and Inflate

Using your thumbs, pop the tire bead back onto the rim. Try to avoid using levers for this step as you might pinch the new tube. Once seated, inflate to the recommended pressure, reinstall the wheel, and you're ready to ride!
